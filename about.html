Career Goals

I want to build systems that sense what is physically happening and turn it into a decision someone can act on: both the embedded hardware that produces the data and the analytics that make it mean something. Those two halves are usually treated as separate jobs. The work I care about sits where they meet, close enough to the hardware to know what a signal really is and close enough to the analysis to know what it is worth. Wiring a robot and building the scouting pipeline that decided how it played turned out to be the same problem, and that is the one I want to keep working on.

This fall I joined Smart Stadium, a Vertically Integrated Projects team at Georgia Tech that instruments Bobby Dodd Stadium for 50,000 fans: a sensor network reading crowd activity, plays, structural vibration and network congestion, with embedded systems, machine learning and wireless sensor network groups building on top of it. It is the scouting system again, at a scale I could not build alone. VIP teams run across years rather than a single semester, which is most of the appeal. I would rather go deep on one system over several semesters than sample a new project each term.

My second thread, Cybersecurity & Privacy, comes from the same instinct. Every robot I have worked on assumes that every message on its bus is honest, and a stadium sensor network does not get to assume that either. Testing battery crimps taught me the small version: the failure was not the battery, it was a joint nobody had measured. A system behaving exactly as built and not at all as intended is also the shape of most security problems, and analytics you cannot trust the inputs to is just confident guessing.
