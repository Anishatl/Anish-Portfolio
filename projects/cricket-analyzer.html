Cricket Bowling Risk Analyzer

Personal machine learning project, 2025

MEDIAPIPE POSE TRACKINGSTREAMLIT96% MODEL ACCURACY

After a bowling injury sidelined me from cricket for months, I taught myself machine learning at a Harvard AI bootcamp and set out to build a tool that could flag risky bowling mechanics before they caused injury.

Building the model

I spent hours talking with Level 2 and Level 3 ICC-recognized cricket coaches — several of whom had played for and coached top-ranked cricket nations — to understand what makes a bowling action risky, and the safe ranges for spine, knee, shoulder, and elbow angles, along with hip-to-shoulder separation.

Using Google's MediaPipe framework, I extracted joint-angle vectors from bowling videos, then ran into a hard problem: there simply weren't enough clean bowling videos publicly available to train a reliable model. My first attempt at synthetic data generation was so clean that a model trained on it hit 99.9969% accuracy — a clear sign of overfitting rather than a good model. I rebuilt the generator to inject controlled randomness that mimicked camera noise and natural variation in bowling form, which brought the model down to a much more trustworthy 96% accuracy.

The app

The final app, built in Streamlit, gives an overall risk summary first, then lets you step through the uploaded video frame-by-frame to see the pose estimation and risk feedback in real time, plus a full-video analysis mode using a separate clip-level model.

Using this tool on my own bowling action helped me return to bowling 25% longer and 6% faster before ultimately stepping back from competitive cricket.
