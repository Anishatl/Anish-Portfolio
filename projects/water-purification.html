Low-Cost Water Purification Device

Science fair project, co-built with David · 2024

$2.41 PER UNITWON SCHOOL SCIENCE FAIR3D PRINTED

After watching my family in India drink turbid well water, I set out to build an affordable water filtration device with my friend David, aiming for something effective enough to matter and cheap enough to actually reach the people who need it.

Research and testing

We narrowed our search to eight filtration methods across three categories — semipermeable membranes for reverse osmosis, wood charcoal for carbon adsorption, and volcanic ash/zeolites for ion exchange — and tested each against E. coli, bacteria, heavy metals, and a range of chemical measures, using water test strips and specialized testing products from partner companies.

Our final combination — a 5-micron semipermeable membrane, hickory wood carbon adsorption, and zeolite/volcanic-ash ion exchange, filtered through cheesecloth — matched or beat tap water on most tested parameters.

Design and cost

We 3D-printed a compact, mass-producible housing with two replaceable filtration cartridges and one membrane compartment, iterating through more than 10 CAD versions before landing on a design that balanced durability, ease of production, and cost. The final device cost $2.41 to produce — over ten times cheaper than a typical water filter — and won our school's science fair.
